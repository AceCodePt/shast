## Reasoning

Comparing the computed style of `root>mid>leaf` across the two states:

- **Normal state:** `padding: 2px 2px 2px 2px`
- **Hover state:** `padding: 2px 2px 2px 2px`

The padding is identical (2px on all sides) in both the normal and hover states. Furthermore, the element's height remains `23.1875px` in both cases — if the `:hover` rule's `padding: 40px` had applied, the top and bottom padding alone would add 80px, making the height much larger.

This tells us the `:hover` block is not winning the cascade. The most likely cause is a **specificity problem**: the rule setting the base `padding: 2px` has higher specificity (or equal specificity with later source order) than the `:hover` rule, so the `40px` value is overridden and never reflected in the computed style.

ANSWER: NO
